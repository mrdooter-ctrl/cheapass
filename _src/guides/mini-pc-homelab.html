Cheap mini PCs for a home server

An old laptop with ambitions. That's what you're buying.

A mini PC uses a fraction of the power of a tower and runs Home Assistant, Pi-hole, Plex or a few virtual machines without complaint. Pick by the job, not by the spec sheet.

Pick by the job

  • Home Assistant, Pi-hole, small stuff: the cheapest box here is plenty.
  • Plex or Jellyfin for the family: an Intel chip. Intel's built-in video engine (Quick Sync) handles transcoding without breaking a sweat. Plex needs Plex Pass for that. Jellyfin does it free.
  • Proxmox with several VMs: more cores and more RAM. RAM runs out before the CPU does.

Before you buy

  • Barebone means no RAM and no SSD. Cheaper up front, but budget for RAM and an SSD on top — compare 1TB, 2TB and 4TB drives on our internal M.2 NVMe SSD variant ladder.
  • Check the network port. 2.5GbE is nice if your NAS has it too — upgrade older boxes with 2.5GbE USB Ethernet adapters under $30 or add ports with budget switches and Cat6 cables.
  • Mini PCs are loud under load on some models. Hide it behind a screen or quiet it down with VESA mounts and quiet USB fans, and keep an HDMI dummy plug handy for headless 4K remote desktop.

Under-$30 setup essentials: USB-C hubs, high-endurance boot drives, precision toolkits and thermal paste, USB-to-SATA cables and SD readers, smart plugs, and cable management.

Hardware fix BOMs: M.2 NVMe clearance & cooling, Proxmox 2.5GbE USB NICs, VESA mounts & cable cleanup. Storage for media goes on a NAS, not the mini PC's little SSD.
