Which UPS your NAS needs

The cheapest insurance in the whole setup.

A UPS is a battery between the wall and your NAS. When the power cuts, it keeps the box alive long enough to shut down cleanly. Without one, a storm can corrupt a volume mid-write. Rebuilding that costs more than the UPS.

Size it in two minutes

  1. Find your NAS power draw. The spec sheet lists it. Add roughly 5 to 8 watts per spinning 3.5" drive if it doesn't include them.
  2. Add 30% headroom. Drives pull more when they spin up.
  3. Runtime: you only need 5 to 10 minutes. The UPS's job is a clean shutdown, not keeping Plex on during a blackout.

Example: a 2-bay NAS at about 20 watts with drives needs a UPS rated for 26 watts or more. Almost any small unit covers that. Not sure what your box actually pulls at the wall? Check it first with plug-in Kill A Watt-style power meters and surge strips or energy-monitoring smart plugs, and tame the power bricks with under-$30 cable management.

The one feature that matters

A USB cable from the UPS to the NAS. That's how the NAS knows the power is out and shuts itself down. Check that your NAS supports your UPS before you buy.

What to buy
