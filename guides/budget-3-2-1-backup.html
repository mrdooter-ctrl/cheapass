The cheapest backup setup that actually works

Three copies. Two kinds of storage. One somewhere else.

That's the 3-2-1 rule. It sounds fussy until a drive dies, a laptop gets stolen or a kid deletes the wedding photos. Here it is at three budgets.

Budget 1: one laptop, no NAS

  • Copy 1: the laptop.
  • Copy 2: an external USB drive, plugged in once a week — compare 1TB, 2TB and 4TB tiers on our portable USB-C SSD variant ladder, or drop a spare M.2 SSD into one of these cheap 10Gbps NVMe enclosures for cold backups.
  • Copy 3: a cloud backup plan for that computer. See yearly vs lifetime.

Cheapest setup that's still real. The weak spot is you remembering to plug the drive in.

Budget 2: a family, a cheap NAS

  • Copy 1: everyone's devices.
  • Copy 2: a 2-bay NAS with two mirrored drives, backing up every device automatically.
  • Copy 3: the NAS backs up its important folders to the cloud, or to a USB drive you keep at work or a relative's house.

Add a small UPS (compare models on our 425VA–1500VA UPS variant ladder). It's the cheapest part and saves the most grief.

Budget 3: a home server

  • Copy 1: a mini PC running your services.
  • Copy 2: a 4-bay NAS holding the data and nightly backups of the mini PC.
  • Copy 3: off-site cloud storage for the folders you can't replace. Photos first, movies last.

The mistakes that cost the most

  • Thinking a mirror is a backup. It copies your mistakes instantly.
  • Never testing a restore. Once a year, pull one file back from each copy.
  • Backing up everything to the cloud. Back up what you can't replace. Movies you can download again.
