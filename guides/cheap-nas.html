Cheapest NAS worth buying

Pick the bays first. The box is the easy part.

A NAS is a small computer full of hard drives that sits on your network. It holds your photos, backs up your laptops and runs Plex if you want it to. The box is a one-time cost. The drives are the real bill.

The one question: how many bays?

  • 2 bays: two drives mirrored. If one dies, the other has everything. You get the space of one drive. Right for most homes.
  • 4 bays: room to add drives later instead of buying a new box. Pick this if your photo library is already a problem.
  • 6 bays or more: only if you already know why. Big media libraries, lots of 4K.

Rule of thumb: count the space you use today, double it, and buy the bays that hold that with one drive to spare.

Things that save money later

  • Buy two drives, not one. A NAS with one drive is a single point of failure with a fan.
  • Plex needs a decent chip. Transcoding video wants an Intel or AMD chip with hardware video support. ARM boxes are fine for files, not for that.
  • A mirror is not a backup. Delete a file and it's gone from both drives. Keep one copy somewhere else. Here's the cheap way.
  • Add a UPS. A power cut mid-write can corrupt a volume. Which one you need — and check your idle draw with plug-in watt meters and surge strips.
  • Wire it cleanly (under $30): add ports with gigabit switches and Cat6 cables, terminate custom runs with Cat6 patch packs and cable testers, and tidy power bricks with budget cable management.
